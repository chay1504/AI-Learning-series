# Forensic Style DNA Report: Tech Boy Deepak, Case-Study & Breakdown Videos

**Purpose.** This report takes apart how Deepak researches, structures, tells and voices his "breakdown" videos, so a new script on any topic can be written that sounds and flows like him. It covers his story arc, research habits, how he uses numbers, his rhetorical moves, his Telugu, his calls to action, his pacing and his packaging. It ends with a checklist and a list of what **not** to copy.

**Evidence base.** Three videos, all full transcripts (Telugu auto-captions plus English translation) in [`../transcripts/`](../transcripts):

## 1. Executive summary: the 12 genes of a Deepak breakdown

1. **Open on a paradox or a shock, never a greeting.** The first line is a question that sounds impossible ("profits are rising but jobs are falling, why?") or a stat dump ("2.3 trillion dollars wiped in one day"). There's no "hi guys" and no channel intro.
2. **Numbers do the persuading.** That's 4.5 to 7.8 numbers per minute in the case-study videos: dated stock moves, quarterly results, before/after ratios, "X times" growth. Every claim gets a figure.
3. **He clarifies inline.** The moment a jargon term appears he stops and says "let me give you a small clarity: X means…" (stocks falling, bubble, ROI, onsite/offshore, time-and-material billing).
4. **A "then what happened?" chain.** History is told as cause and effect. Each step ends with "ఆ తర్వాత ఏమైంది?" or "then what happened?" and leads into the next.
5. **The twist beat.** At least once per video: "ఇక్కడ ట్విస్ట్ ఏంటి అంటే…" or "here's the real twist". A fact is set up as positive, then flipped.
6. **One big local analogy per video, from Hyderabad or family life.** The Ameerpet biryani hotel with loan EMIs, the mestri (mason) and his workers as a company pyramid, Jio's free data in 2016. It runs about 60 to 90 seconds and is mapped back explicitly: "exactly the same thing in AI…".
7. **An India angle is mandatory.** A global story is always brought home: Indian users, Indian companies, ₹ crore and lakh figures, Visakhapatnam, "your babai or peddananna went to the US".
8. **He keeps asking the viewer to think.** That's over 2 questions per minute: "just think once", "now I'm asking all of you one question", "do you know which companies came forward?", plus quiz prompts ("tell me, what is Gemini's latest model?").
9. **He corrects the myth.** He states what "a lot of people think" and then corrects it: "AI will take all our jobs", "the bubble means AI is dead", "knowing ChatGPT means knowing AI".
10. **The ending has a precedent, a watch-list and an action.** History says X (the dot-com crash, yet the internet survived). Here are the dates or signals to watch. Here's what *you* should do.
11. **A peer-mentor persona.** He speaks as a big brother ("anna") who visits colleges, talks to founders, and "made a script for you". He swears honesty ("not one thing I say is made up") and mentions his team's effort.
12. **Telangana-flavoured Tenglish.** Telugu grammar carries English nouns, numbers and verbs (ఇన్వెస్ట్ చేసినారు, గ్రో అయినాయి). He uses Telangana verb endings (-inaaru, -indu, -tadi) and three tics: అన్నమాట, కదా, అంటే.

### 2.2 Time budget (case-study format, normalised to about 18 to 20 minutes)

| Block | Share of runtime | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open + stakes + promise (B1 to B2) | 5–8% (about 60–90 s) | Stats come first, the promise second, all inside 75 s |
| Early CTA (B4) | 1–2% (about 20 s) | Either near 0:45 or at the midpoint. Never twice in the first half |
| Concept + origin (B5 to B7) | 15–20% | One "I still remember that date" |
| Escalation + mechanism (B8 to B10) | 35–40% | The dense, number-heavy middle |
| Analogy (B11) | 7–10% | One long one plus one short callback analogy |
| India angle (B12) | 8–12% | Either its own block or woven through |
| Myth / precedent / watch-list / action (B13 to B16) | 15–20% | The resolution |
| Recap + close (B17 to B18) | 4–6% | Always ends on a question to comment on |

**Pacing.** He speaks fast: about 167 to 174 Telugu caption-words a minute, which comes to about 200 to 220 words a minute in the English translation. A beat changes about every 60 to 75 seconds. He never stays on a single number for more than one sentence unless it is the hook number.

### 2.3 Arc shape (emotion)

`Shock / fear (0–2 min) → curiosity (history) → rising tension (numbers keep getting bigger) → twist → understanding (analogy) → reassurance ("it's not the end") → empowerment (what you do now)`

He scares first, explains second and reassures last. He never ends on doom. Even "Is AI dead?" ends with "AI isn't going anywhere; AI is growing."

## 3. Research DNA: what he looks up and how he uses it

### 3.1 Source types he relies on (in order of frequency)

1. **Quarterly results and company filings:** HCL net income 3,843 Cr → 4,624 Cr (+20.3%), headcount changes, Infosys large-deal value $3.6B, Microsoft profit +32%, Azure +48%, Nvidia $108B in a quarter.
2. **Dated market moves:** "Thursday, Nov 5: $2.3T wiped from Nasdaq-100", "Oct 29: Alphabet −13%, worst day since its 2004 listing", "Intel −28.6% in a day".
3. **Adoption and scale metrics:** 1B weekly ChatGPT users, 180M Indian monthly users, 100M users in 2 months vs Instagram's 2.5 years.
4. **CEO / authority quotes:** Dario Amodei (white-collar jobs), IBM's Arvind Krishna ($8T capex needs $800B a year in profit), Bill Clinton (Y2K).
5. **Historical precedent:** the dot-com crash (Nasdaq −78%), Y2K (1998 to 2000), Jio 2016.
6. **India-specific data:** India AI Mission (38,000 GPUs), Google's $15B in Visakhapatnam, FII flows (₹15,000 Cr in, ₹3 lakh Cr out), Nifty, the TCS fresher-ratio presentation (45:55 → 60:40), the 23 lakh vs 12 lakh AI-jobs gap.
7. **Industry mechanics:** time-and-material billing (40 people × 160 hours = 6,400 hours), onsite vs offshore rates ($67 vs $27 an hour), the levels pyramid.

### 3.2 How a fact is packaged (the "number sandwich")

Almost every number follows this pattern:

> **Context** ("HCL Tech, you know the company, right?") → **number pair, before → after** ("3,843 Cr to 4,624 Cr") → **derived meaning** ("that means 20.3% growth in just one year") → **twist** ("but here's the real twist: did jobs also go up?")

Patterns to reuse:
- **Before → after across years:** "2019: $70B → 2026: $725B, more than 10× in seven years".
- **Per-unit ratio:** "for every dollar they earn, they spend three".
- **Speed comparison:** "100M users in 2 months; Instagram took two and a half years".
- **Ranking superlative:** "worst day since 2004", "first company ever at $5T", "one of the biggest foreign investments in history".
- **Rupee conversion and Indian units:** "3 lakh crore, *literally* three lakh crore", "₹1 lakh profit a month". He says large Indian numbers twice for impact.
- **Daily-life price impact:** "laptops up 15–30%, mobiles up 10–20%". He links macro news to the viewer's wallet.

### 3.3 Research depth and selection rules (inferred)

- **Recency is king.** Most stats are from the last 1 to 4 weeks, with exact dates ("last Thursday, November 5th"). He films with a date stamp: "today is November 8".
- **About three companies per claim.** HCL, then Tech Mahindra, then Infosys; or Google, then Intel. Each one adds a new angle.
- **He works out the "so what".** He rarely leaves a raw number. He computes a ratio or a "that means".
- **He doesn't cite on screen.** He says "according to a 2008 TCS presentation" or "news came recently" but doesn't name the outlet. (Our research docs **must** keep sources, even if the script doesn't read them out.)
- **Weak spot: arithmetic.** See §11. Our process adds a maths check.

## 5. Explanation technique (the micro-loop)

For every new concept he runs this 5-step loop. It is the core teaching unit:

1. **Name it as a question.** "What exactly is a bubble?" or "What is onsite?"
2. **Give a simple definition** in one sentence, often with "simple ga cheppali ante".
3. **Make it concrete** with a number or a mini-scenario ("suppose a project needs 500 employees…").
4. **Use an analogy** if it is abstract (biryani, mestri, Jio).
5. **Map it back and give the "so what"**: "Exactly the same thing in AI…" or "Why should you know these two words? Because…"

**Depth dial.** He explains business mechanics (billing, pyramids, capex vs revenue, circular money) to a "smart 20-year-old" level. He never uses formulas beyond multiplication and percentages. Each jargon term gets exactly one plain-language gloss and is then used freely.

## 6. Voice and language DNA

### 6.1 Register

- **Spoken Telangana/Hyderabadi Telugu with heavy English mixing.** Nouns, numbers, company names, units and most technical or business verbs are English. Grammar, connectors and endings are Telugu.
- **Numbers are said in English** (ఫైవ్ ట్రిలియన్ డాలర్స్, 2.3 ట్రిలియన్ డాలర్స్), except Indian units (లక్ష, కోట్లు / క్రోర్స్, both appear).
- **English verbs plus a Telugu light verb:** ఇన్వెస్ట్ చేసినారు, గ్రో అయినాయి, లాంచ్ చేస్తున్నాం, రీప్లేస్ అవుతున్నాయి, స్టాండ్ అవుట్ అవుతారు.

### 6.4 Sentence rhythm

- Sentences are long, run on, and joined with అంటే / సో / కదా. Each one ends in a restatement.
- **Repetition for emphasis:** "AI, AI, AI", "we'll hire, we'll hire", "important, important story", "do it quick, quick", "big, big projects". Doubled adjectives (పెద్ద పెద్ద, చిన్న చిన్న).
- **Self-correction on air:** "on this, I mean…", "what kind of skill should I mention… suppose sales". It keeps the delivery unscripted-sounding even though he works from a script.
- **Inclusive "మనం / మనందరం"** (we / all of us) when building shared stakes. **"మీరు"** (you) for the action steps.

### 6.5 English-script equivalent

For the English version of a script, keep the **same moves** in Indian-English register: "right?", "I mean", "basically", "just think once", "here's the real twist", "so to put it simply", "bro" (rarely), "literally", "very, very important", "you got clarity, right?". Keep ₹ crore and lakh. Short spoken sentences, no essay prose.

## 7. Persona and tone

- **Big-brother mentor (అన్న).** Students call him anna, founders ask him for interns, he visits colleges. He is at the same level as the viewer, just better informed.
- **Urgent but caring.** Fear is the hook ("you will definitely fall behind"), but the promise is protection ("100% safety", "your job isn't going away").
- **Confident but not academic.** He offers his opinion freely ("as far as I know"), and asks the audience for theirs ("what do you think? Tell me in the comments").
- **Hyderabadi identity, said openly:** "Hyderabadi style lo cheppali ante", Ameerpet, biryani, Vizag, "our Andhra Pradesh".
- **Effort and honesty claims** build trust: "I came with a script", "nothing made up", "my team and I worked one week".

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Alarm emoji + provocative claim + "Why ??"** | "🚨 No more IT jobs in India ❗ Why ?? \| Detailed Explanation 2026" |
| **Yes/no question + urgency** | "Is AI DEAD ? 😱😱 \| Watch this immediately." |
| **Number + deadline** | "🔥 📈 5 Skills To Learn Before 2027 \| 70 days to GO #2027" |

- English titles (even though the video is in Telugu), 2 to 3 emojis, a "\|" separator, a promise suffix ("Detailed Explanation", "Watch this immediately").
- **Performance signal:** the fear-plus-current-event question ("Is AI DEAD?") pulled about 4× the views of the listicle and about 2.4× the IT-jobs case study within similar windows. A **provocative yes/no or "Why?" title on a big news topic** is his strongest format.

## 10. Format variants

| Variant | Arc used | Density | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Case study / industry breakdown** | Full B1 to B18, with an agenda ("three things") | 6 to 8 numbers a minute, history-heavy | 03 IT Jobs |
| **Current-event explainer** | B1 to B18, with a watch-list of dates | 4 to 5 numbers a minute, very recent dates | 01 Is AI Dead |
| **Listicle / motivation** | B1, B2, B3, B4, then N × (name → why → example → action), then B16, B18 | About 2 numbers a minute, stories over stats | 02 5 Skills |

**A company or ecosystem breakdown** (like the Elon Musk video) is a **case-study variant**: history-heavy origin, an escalation chain across companies, a twist ("he isn't building companies…"), one big local analogy, an India angle, a precedent, a watch-list and a viewer takeaway.

## 11. Forensic flags: what NOT to copy

These came up during transcription. A script "in his style" should keep the voice but **fix** these:

| Issue | Where | Policy for our scripts |
|---|---|---|
| **Arithmetic slips** | "For every dollar they spend three" (12 / 6.7 ≈ 1.8); "loss of $12B" (that was the spending; the loss is about $5.3B); "6400 × 2" | Run a maths check on every derived number |
| **Inconsistent numbers** | 70 vs 75 vs 55 days; "next 15 minutes" in a 12-minute video; capex $700B then $725B | Use one number per fact, consistently |
| **Payoff without a plant** | "I told you at the start to remember the dates" (he didn't) | Every callback needs an explicit plant earlier in the script |
| **Unsourced claims** | "news came recently", "according to a presentation" | The spoken script can stay casual, but the research doc must give the source and as-of date for every fact |
| **Profit vs revenue confusion** | Anthropic "profit of $17B" (likely revenue) | Use finance terms precisely: revenue, profit, valuation, market cap |
| **Over-promising certainty** | "100% safety for your job" | Keep the confidence, drop absolute guarantees about money, jobs or stocks |
| **Future events stated as fact** | "Anthropic IPO in the next few weeks" | Frame these as "reports say", "expected", "planned" |

## 12. Scriptwriter's checklist (one page)

**Research**
- [ ] 1 hook paradox or shock stat, from the last 1 to 4 weeks, with an exact date.
- [ ] 3 to 6 rapid stats for the cold open.
- [ ] An origin date the presenter can "still remember".
- [ ] For each major claim, numbers from three companies or examples, each adding an angle.
- [ ] Before → after pairs, per-unit ratios, a speed comparison, superlatives.
- [ ] An India angle: Indian users, ₹ figures, an Indian place or company, a government programme.
- [ ] One historical precedent.
- [ ] A watch-list of 2 or 3 upcoming dates or signals.
- [ ] Every number has a source and an as-of date in the research doc, and passes the maths check.

**Structure**
- [ ] No greeting. The first line is a question or a shock.
- [ ] "Watch till the end" plus the scope inside the first 75 s.
- [ ] One CTA block, either early (about 0:45) or mid-roll (about 48%), with the 90%/99% guilt line.
- [ ] An agenda ("today we learn three things") for case studies.
- [ ] The "then what happened?" chain through the history.
- [ ] At least one "here's the twist".
- [ ] One "X entered the story; now the whole story changes" turn.
- [ ] One big Hyderabadi or family analogy (60 to 90 s), mapped back explicitly, plus one short callback analogy.
- [ ] At least one myth correction ("a lot of people think…").
- [ ] Precedent → watch-list → viewer action → recap → comment poll → targeted share → topic request → sign-off.

**Voice**
- [ ] Telangana verb forms (-inaaru, -indu, -tadi); అంటే / కదా / సో / అన్నమాట throughout.
- [ ] Numbers in English words; Indian units for ₹; large figures said twice ("3 lakh crore, literally three lakh crore").
- [ ] More than 2 direct questions a minute; at least one quiz or "think once" moment.
- [ ] Ventriloquise at least one stakeholder ("the client says…", "investors thought…").
- [ ] Doubling for emphasis ("big, big", "AI, AI, AI").
- [ ] Ends in reassurance and empowerment, never doom.

**Length targets (case study):** 18 to 20 minutes; about 3,600 to 4,200 English words in the translation register, or about 3,000 to 3,400 Telugu words; 5 to 7 numbers a minute; a new beat every 60 to 75 s.
