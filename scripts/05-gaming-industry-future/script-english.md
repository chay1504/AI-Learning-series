## [00:50–01:55] Part 1 · How a game makes money (GTA 6)

First, let's understand how a game even makes this much money. Because you pay just ₹5,999 for GTA 6 in India.

First, pre-booking. 32.5 million copies were sold before the game even released. Second, the sale itself, but Sony or Microsoft usually take around 30% of every digital copy. And there's no disc, even in the box; it's just a download code.

> On screen: 32.5M pre-sold · ₹5,999 / $79.99 · ~30% platform cut · box = download code

Third, and this is the real money: online. In GTA Online you buy Shark Cards with real money. The biggest one costs 99.99 dollars for 8 million fake GTA dollars. According to leaked internal data, GTA Online was making around 1.32 million dollars a day, and only around 4% of players were paying. That's why 78% of Take-Two's money comes from in-game spending, not from selling games.

And fourth, the long tail. GTA 5 came out in 2013, and it's still selling around 54,000 copies every single day, nearly 235 million copies total. And no ads inside the game: zero real brands, only fake ones like "Sprunk" for Sprite.

> On screen: Shark Card $99.99 = GTA$8M · leaked: ~$1.32M/day, ~4% pay · GTA 5 ~235M copies, ~54,000/day · no in-game ads

To put it simply: GTA isn't selling you a CD. It's selling you a SIM card. You buy it once and keep recharging for years. And this is just one game. Now let's zoom out.

That means gaming is around 6 times bigger than the entire world's movie box office. Around 3.6 billion people play games, almost every second human. And analysts expect around 234 billion dollars by 2028, and PwC says around 300 billion by 2029 if you include ads.

> On screen: games $201.6B vs global box office $33.6B (≈ 6×) · 3.6B players · $234B by 2028 (Newzoo) · ~$300B by 2029 (PwC)

Now look at the companies. Tencent from China made around 9.3 billion dollars from games in just one quarter, three months. That's more than Take-Two, the owner of GTA, makes in a whole year. Sony's PlayStation business makes around 31 billion dollars a year. Microsoft paid 69 billion dollars to buy Activision Blizzard, the makers of Call of Duty, the biggest gaming deal in history. And EA, the FIFA and Battlefield company, was bought for 55 billion dollars this year.

> On screen: Tencent games ~$9.3B in ONE quarter · Sony PlayStation ~$31B/year · Microsoft–Activision $69B · EA $55B

And how many people work in one gaming company? Sony's PlayStation business has around 12,300 employees. Take-Two has around 12,900. Ubisoft, the Assassin's Creed company, has around 16,600. Nintendo's Switch 2 sold 26 million consoles in about 16 months, and Roblox has 138 million people playing every single day.

And the stock market moves with games. Every time GTA 6 got delayed, Take-Two's stock fell around 7 to 10% in a day. When the reviews came out with a 99 score, it went up around 6%. And even giants bleed: Xbox cut around 3,200 jobs this year, about 20% of its staff.

> On screen: employees: Sony PlayStation ~12,300 · Take-Two ~12,900 · Ubisoft ~16,600 · Switch 2: 26M in ~16 months · Roblox 138M daily users · Take-Two stock −7–10% per GTA delay · Xbox −3,200 jobs (~20%)

And how much does one game cost? Guess once. A typical big AAA game now costs around 300 million dollars or more. The Last of Us Part 2 cost around 220 million and took almost 6 years. One Call of Duty game cost more than 700 million dollars. Red Dead Redemption 2 has 3,024 names in its credits. > On screen: typical AAA ~$300M+ · The Last of Us Part II ~$220M, ~6 yrs · Call of Duty $700M+ · RDR2: 3,024 names in credits · GTA 6: ~5,900 devs (reported), biggest team Rockstar India (reported)

## [04:15–05:10] Part 2b · The roles inside a game studio

And a lot of people think, "Game industry means only coding." That's not it. A big game needs a whole army.

**Design:** game designers decide what's fun, level designers build the maps and missions, systems designers balance weapons and skills, narrative designers write the story, UX designers design the menus, and economy designers design the in-game money.

**Engineering:** gameplay programmers, engine programmers, graphics programmers, network programmers for multiplayer, and AI programmers for enemies and NPCs.

**Art**, usually the biggest department, around 30 to 50% of the team: concept artists, 3D environment and character artists, animators, VFX artists for explosions and magic, and technical artists, who connect art and code and are among the hardest people to hire.

**Then:** sound designers and composers, producers who ship the game, QA testers who find the bugs, and after launch, LiveOps, data analysts, community managers, and localisation. Yes, someone has to translate the whole game into Telugu too.

> On screen: role map: Design · Engineering · Art (largest, ~30–50%) · Audio · Production · QA · LiveOps / Data / Community / Localisation

So whether you're a coder, an artist, a writer, a musician or a data person, there's a seat for you in gaming.

## [05:10–07:05] Part 3 · India and Unleash the Avatar

Now let's come to India. India has more than 50 crore gamers, more than the entire population of America. But India's game spending is only around 1.5 billion dollars, less than 1% of the world. Lumikai expects it to grow to 3.2 billion dollars by 2030.

> On screen: India 50 crore+ gamers · ~$1.5B (2025) → $3.2B (2030, Lumikai) · <1% of global spend

And jobs? The government's own AVGC task force says India needs around 20 lakh more people in animation, VFX, gaming and comics by 2030. And according to one industry estimate, India has only around 15,000 game developers, compared with around 58 lakh software developers. Just think about that gap once.

> On screen: need 20 lakh more AVGC people by 2030 · ~15,000 game devs vs ~58 lakh software devs

And Indian studios are trying something big. Let me tell you about one: Aeos Games, from Bengaluru, co-founded by Varun Mayya, with his brother Rohan Mayya as CEO. A team of just 40 people, completely self-funded, making a game called *Unleash the Avatar*: a soulslike action game based on Indian epics. The hero, Vikram, fights Asuras and Rakshasas as hell leaks into the mortal world, and he can transform into avatar forms. The hero's voice is Johnny Yong Bosch, a famous American voice actor.

> On screen: Aeos Games, Bengaluru · co-founders Varun Mayya & Rohan Mayya (CEO) · 40 people · self-funded · *Unleash the Avatar* (planned 2026, wishlist on Steam)

But the most interesting part is a technique called photogrammetry. Simply put, you take hundreds of photos of a real object, like a temple pillar or a whole street, from every angle, and software turns it into a 3D model with real textures. You literally photocopy reality into the game. That's why Epic Games bought Quixel and RealityCapture, the big photogrammetry companies. And Aeos says it got permission from the Archaeological Survey of India to scan an entire town, Chanderi in Madhya Pradesh, and has more than 1,000 scanned assets. The game is planned for 2026, and you can wishlist it on Steam.

> On screen: photogrammetry: photos → 3D model · Epic bought Quixel (2019) & RealityCapture (2021) · Aeos (studio claim): ASI permission to scan Chanderi, 1,000+ scanned assets

And why does this matter? Look at China. Black Myth: Wukong is based on *Journey to the West*, a centuries-old story. It scanned real temples, and 27 of its 36 real locations are in one province, Shanxi. After the game launched, visitors to those sites went up 156%. A game became tourism. And Ghost of Tsushima, a game about Japanese samurai culture, was made by an American studio and sold more than 13 million copies.

> On screen: Black Myth: Wukong: 27 of 36 locations in Shanxi · visitors +156% · Ghost of Tsushima, US studio, Japanese culture, 13M+

We have the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and thousands of temples. If we don't make games on our stories, someone else will.

## [07:05–08:35] Part 4 · Why gaming, and why now

So why should someone choose gaming over Bollywood, a digital marketing agency, or a regular startup?

Look at films. Our film industry made a record ₹20,500 crore, but digital and satellite rights values actually fell 8 to 10% last year. A film earns most of its money in a few weeks. Now look at GTA 5: still selling 13 years later.

To put it in Hyderabadi style: a film is like a big wedding function. Huge crowd for one day, and then it's over. A good game is like a biryani hotel that's been running for 50 years. It keeps serving, every single day.

> On screen: film = wedding function (one big day) · game = 50-year-old biryani hotel (serves for decades) · Indian film digital/satellite rights −8–10% (2025)

A digital marketing agency is a big market, but it's a service. You bill clients by the hour, and you don't own anything you build. A game is IP: you build it once and own it forever. That's exactly the shift Aeos made. Its parent group started in media and services, and now it's building its own IP. Their own line is: "The future of content is gaming."

And startups? In 2026, Indian startup funding grew 7%, but the number of deals fell 38% and seed funding fell 37%. It's very hard for first-time founders. But with games, a small team can put a game on Steam or Roblox and sell to the whole world from day one.

> On screen: India startups 2026: funding +7% · deals −38% · seed −37% (Tracxn) · games: Steam/Roblox = global market from day one

So why now? Because the talent gap is huge, the engines like Unreal and Unity are free to start with, thousands of scanned assets are free, Indian mythology is completely untapped, and AI is cutting the cost of making games. But honestly, salaries in India are still low at the start, around ₹3 to 6 lakh, layoffs happen, and hits are rare. Go in with skill, not just passion.

## [08:35–11:15] Part 5 · How AI is changing gaming

And now the most important part: AI.

First, making games. In one Google Cloud survey, 87% of game developers said they already use AI agents. Morgan Stanley estimates AI could cut the cost of making a game by nearly half, worth around 22 billion dollars a year in extra profit for the industry. Square Enix, the makers of Final Fantasy, wants AI to do 70% of its bug testing by the end of 2027. And Krafton, the company behind BGMI, declared itself an "AI First" company and is spending around 70 million dollars on more than 1,000 GPUs.

> On screen: 87% of devs use AI agents (Google Cloud survey) · AI could cut dev costs ~50%, ~$22B/yr (Morgan Stanley) · Square Enix: 70% of QA by 2027 · Krafton (BGMI): "AI First", ~$70M, 1,000+ GPUs

Second, AI inside games. In Fortnite, players talked to an AI Darth Vader, and they made him swear on day one. In Where Winds Meet, some characters are AI chatbots, and around 2 million players joined in its first weekend. Krafton's inZOI, with AI-driven characters, sold 1 million copies in its first week.

And the craziest: Google released an AI that can generate a playable world from a prompt. The day after it launched, Unity's stock fell 24% in one day and Roblox fell 13%. Investors got scared. But even Google says it's not a game engine. It can't make a full game yet.

> On screen: Fortnite AI Darth Vader · Where Winds Meet, AI NPCs, 2M players · inZOI 1M copies week 1 · Google Project Genie → Unity −24%, Roblox −13% in one day

And on Steam, almost half of all new games released in October 2026 disclosed that they used AI, up from less than 2% in early 2024. But here's the twist: among small games with fewer than 10 reviews, 47% use AI, while among big games with 1,000-plus reviews, only 14% do. AI is flooding the market with small games. Quality still decides who wins.

And the tools are already in your hands. Unity's new AI assistant has an agent mode that can edit your game scene directly. Roblox lets creators generate working 3D objects just by typing. One warning, though: in the US, content made purely by AI can't be copyrighted. So your own creativity is what you actually own.

But there's a backlash too. Clair Obscur, one of the best games of 2025, lost an indie award because it had used AI placeholder textures. Arc Raiders used AI voices and still sold more than 16 million copies, but later re-recorded many lines with real actors, saying, "A real professional actor is better than AI." And voice actors went on strike for 11 months to win rules on AI consent.

> On screen: Steam: 49.9% of new games disclosed AI (Oct 2026) vs 1.7% (Jan 2024) · Clair Obscur lost award over AI textures · Arc Raiders 16M+, AI voices replaced: "A real professional actor is better than AI"

So can you use AI as a catalyst? Yes, but smartly. Pieter Levels had never made a game, and he built a flight simulator demo with AI in about 3 hours, and it went viral. But reports say many solo AI game-makers earn very little. Why? Because AI gives you speed. It doesn't give you fun, taste or an audience.

So here's how to use AI:
- Prototype your idea in a weekend.
- Use AI for code and placeholder art.
- Keep the story, the art direction and the soul human.
- Be honest about AI use.
- Start building your audience from day one, on Roblox, Steam or YouTube.

That's how a small Indian team can do what 500-person studios did 10 years ago.
