## 2. Launch money: official vs estimates

### OFFICIAL (Take-Two / Rockstar)
- **$3.79B "day one retail sales"**, company estimates through end of 19 Nov 2026. Called "the biggest entertainment launch in history", beating every movie opening. [V] https://variety.com/2026/gaming/news/gta-6-sales-billions-first-day-1236954198/
- **32.5M units sold pre-release**, a record across all platforms. [V] (same release)
- Sam Houser (Rockstar president and founder) is quoted calling it "a new chapter" for Rockstar. [S] https://www.technobezz.com/news/gta-6-sales-rockstar-launch-record
- Metacritic 99 is cited in the release. [S]

**Does the $3.79B include pre-orders?** It is not fully clear, so phrase it carefully on camera. [U]
- The release lists the 32.5M pre-release units separately from the $3.79B figure. Some outlets (Technobezz, The News PK, Wccftech) describe the $3.79B as "pre-orders + first-day sales". The most likely reading is that the 32.5M pre-sold copies are part of the day-one sell-through, since pre-orders are recognised when the game unlocks. Rough check: 32.5M × $79.99 ≈ **$2.6B** at Standard list price, and the Ultimate mix plus launch-day buyers could plausibly reach $3.79B. This is an inference, not stated by Take-Two.
- "Retail sales" here almost certainly means **consumer spend** (what buyers paid, across all stores), **not Take-Two's revenue**. Sony and Microsoft keep a platform cut (~30% on digital), and retailers take a margin on boxed copies. GTA V's 2013 "$800M day one" was the same kind of retail-value number. Take-Two's own *net bookings* will appear in its Q3 FY27 results (Feb 2027). [U, interpretation]
- Safe script line: "Take-Two says gamers spent $3.79 billion on GTA 6 in its first 24 hours, counting the 32.5 million copies people pre-ordered."
