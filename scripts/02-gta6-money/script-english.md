## [01:45–04:00] B7 · Part 1, the origin story

So let's go back. In Dundee, a small city in Scotland, there was a tiny studio called DMA Design. Their big hit was a cute puzzle game called *Lemmings*. And in 1995 they started making a car game called "Race'n'Chase", a game where you could play as a cop or as a criminal.

But here's the interesting part. While testing, they found that catching criminals was boring, and being the criminal was more fun. So they changed the whole game. And in 1997 that game came out with a new name: *Grand Theft Auto*.

> On screen: Dundee, Scotland → DMA Design → *Lemmings* → "Race'n'Chase" → *Grand Theft Auto* (1997)

The game was top-down, very simple graphics. But the controversy was massive. A tabloid PR man stirred up outrage in British newspapers, and the game was even debated in the UK's House of Lords. And all that outrage? It became free advertising.

Now listen to this carefully, because this is the most important number in the origin story. In 1998, an American company called Take-Two bought the publisher of GTA for around 14.2 million dollars in stock. The same year, the Houser brothers, Sam and Dan, started Rockstar Games in New York. And in 1999, Take-Two bought the developer, DMA Design, for just one pound plus around 11.9 million dollars of its debt.

That means Take-Two got GTA, the publisher and the studio together, for around 26 million dollars. Remember this number: 26 million dollars. I'll come back to it.

> On screen: 1998 publisher ~$14.2M + 1999 DMA £1 + ~$11.9M debt = ~$26M

Just think about this once. Twenty-six million dollars. Today some Indian startups raise that much in one funding round. And with that money, Take-Two got the rights to what would become the biggest entertainment franchise on the planet.

Then the story exploded. In 2001, GTA 3 came out on PlayStation 2. It was supposed to come in early October, but after the 9/11 attacks in America they delayed it by a few weeks and changed parts of the game. And when it came, for the first time you could roam a full 3D city freely. That's where the word "open world" really became famous. Then came Vice City, and then San Andreas in 2004, which sold 17.33 million copies on PS2 alone. Till today it's the best-selling PlayStation 2 game ever.

But there were problems too. In 2005, a hidden adult mini-game called "Hot Coffee" was found in San Andreas. The game got an "Adults Only" rating, stores pulled it from shelves, and Take-Two lost around 24.5 million dollars just in returns. Then in 2008, GTA 4 made around 500 million dollars in its first week. Every game was bigger than the last.

## [04:00–06:20] B8 · Part 2, the GTA 5 money machine

And then came the monster: GTA 5, released on September 17, 2013. It reportedly cost around 265 million dollars to make and market, one of the most expensive games ever at that time.

And on day one it made 800 million dollars. In three days it crossed 1 billion dollars, the fastest any entertainment product had ever reached 1 billion. Even the Avengers movie took 19 days.

> On screen: GTA 5: $800M day one · $1B in 3 days (Avengers film: 19 days)

But the real twist isn't the launch. It's what happened after. Let me ask you a small quiz. How many copies of GTA 5 do you think have been sold till now? 50 million? 100 million? Think once and keep a number in your mind.

GTA 5 came out on PS3, a console most of you don't even own anymore. Then it was released on PS4, on PC, on PS5. And today, in 2026, GTA 5 has sold nearly 235 million copies. Two hundred and thirty-five million. Whatever number you guessed, I'm sure it was lower. Even in the last quarter alone it sold 5 million copies, around 54,000 copies every single day, 13 years after launch.

> On screen: GTA 5 ~235M copies · still ~54,000 copies/day (2026)

And in the year that ended in March 2026, GTA products made around 825 million dollars for Take-Two. That's around ₹21 crore every day, from a 13-year-old game, before GTA 6 even came out.

Now how is that possible? The answer is one word: **GTA Online**. In GTA Online you can buy "Shark Cards" with real money. The biggest one, called Megalodon, costs 99.99 dollars, and it gives you 8 million GTA dollars. That means you pay real money for fake money, to buy fake cars, in a fake city. And there's a monthly subscription called GTA+ for 7.99 dollars.

And here's the most shocking part. In April 2026, hackers leaked Rockstar's internal data. Rockstar confirmed there was a breach but didn't confirm the numbers, so take this as leaked data. According to that data, GTA Online was making around 1.32 million dollars a day. And only around 4% of the players were paying anything. Remember this 4%. I'll explain it with a Hyderabad example in a few minutes.

> On screen: Shark Card $99.99 = GTA$8M · GTA+ $7.99/month · leaked: ~$1.32M/day, ~4% of players pay

And this isn't just GTA. For Take-Two as a company, 78% of its money in that year didn't come from selling games. It came from in-game purchases, add-ons and ads, what they call "recurrent consumer spending". That's the real business of gaming today.

And there's one more engine. GTA 5 was the second most-watched game in the whole world in 2025, with around 1.9 billion hours of streams. A big part of that is GTA roleplay on mods like FiveM. And here's a fun fact: Rockstar once banned the team that made FiveM. Eight years later, in 2023, Rockstar bought them. If you can't beat them, buy them.

## [06:30–08:45] B8 · Part 3a, the GTA 6 launch

GTA 6 development reportedly started around 2014. In 2022, an 18-year-old hacker leaked 90 clips of the game, and Rockstar said that hack cost it around 5 million dollars. In December 2023, the first trailer got more than 90 million YouTube views in 24 hours, a world record. And the second trailer in May 2025? Rockstar says 475 million views across platforms in just 24 hours.

> On screen: Trailer 1 · 90M+ YouTube views in 24h · Trailer 2 · 475M views in 24h (Rockstar claim)

Then came the delays. First it was supposed to come in fall 2025. Then May 2026. Then November 2026. And every time Rockstar said "delay", Take-Two's stock fell around 7 to 10% in a single day. That's how much money depends on one game.

Then on June 25, 2026, they announced the price. Everyone was scared it would be 100 dollars. But it came at 79.99 dollars for Standard and 99.99 for Ultimate. And in India? ₹5,999 for Standard and ₹7,499 for Ultimate. Now here's a small twist for Indians. The US Standard price of 79.99 dollars is around ₹7,500 in our money. That means we're paying around ₹1,500 less than Americans. And our Ultimate edition costs almost the same as their Standard edition.

> On screen: US $79.99 (≈ ₹7,519) vs India ₹5,999 · Ultimate ₹7,499

And one more funny thing: even if you buy the "physical" box, there's no disc inside, only a download code. Why? Because Take-Two's CEO says gaming is now "well over 90% digital". Even the box is just a fancy cover for a code.

And Sony went all out. GTA 6 isn't a PlayStation exclusive; it launched on Xbox the same day. But reports say Sony has a big marketing deal, which is why GTA 6 was the first thing you saw when you switched on a PS5.

On November 18 the reviews came out, and GTA 6 got a 99 on Metacritic. That ties it with Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the highest-rated game in history.

And then, November 19, launch day. On launch night, around 7 million people were watching GTA 6 streams live at the same moment. And Take-Two says gamers spent 3.79 billion dollars in that first day. That's almost 5 times GTA 5's first day.

> On screen: GTA 6 day one $3.79B vs GTA 5 $800M (≈ 4.7×) · Metacritic 99

Now remember that 26 million dollars I told you to remember? Take-Two bought the whole GTA publisher and studio for around 26 million dollars. And GTA 6 made around 145 times that in one single day.

And what about the budget? Rockstar never revealed it. But analysts estimate 1 to 1.5 billion dollars to develop, and maybe 2 billion or more with marketing. If those estimates are right, GTA 6 may have earned back its entire budget in one day.

But wait. Does all this 3.79 billion dollars go to Rockstar? No. And this is where it gets interesting.

## [08:45–10:00] B10 · Where your ₹5,999 goes

That 3.79 billion is what gamers spent. It's not what Take-Two keeps. When you buy a digital copy on PlayStation or Xbox, the platform usually takes around 30%. So in a standard industry model, out of an 80-dollar game, around 24 dollars goes to Sony or Microsoft, and around 56 dollars goes to Take-Two, and from that they still have to pay for development, marketing and tax. Rockstar's exact deal isn't public, but that's the standard model.

> On screen: $79.99 digital → ~$24 platform (~30%) · ~$56 Take-Two (industry model)

And which platform? One estimate from Sensor Tower says around 75% of the digital copies were sold on PlayStation. That's why Sony also wins big. PS5 crossed 100 million units sold in November 2026, and reports say one week of PS5 Pro sales in the UK jumped by over 300%.

And who's buying? A research firm called Circana found that 29% of all American console and PC gamers planned to buy GTA 6, the highest purchase intent they have ever recorded for any game. And in Britain, one in three adults has played a GTA game at some point.

But in India the story is different. Business Today estimated that if you don't own a PS5, you need around ₹70,000 to play GTA 6 on day one, console plus game. And there's no PC version yet. So most Indian gamers, who play on mobile and PC, are still just watching it on YouTube. Tell me honestly in the comments: did you buy it, or are you watching streams like most of us?

## [10:00–10:55] B11 · The analogy: Numaish

Now remember that 4%? Let me explain it with something every Hyderabadi knows: Numaish, the big exhibition at Nampally.

The entry ticket for Numaish is small, right? But nobody makes real money from the ticket. The real money is inside: the stalls, the food, the giant wheel, the shopping. And if you observe, most people just walk around, eat one mirchi bajji and go home. But a few families come in and spend thousands. Those few families are what make the stalls rich. And the bigger the crowd, the more crowd it attracts. And the owner of the ground? He takes rent from every single stall.

Exactly the same thing happens with GTA.

> On screen: mapping table:
> - Entry ticket → the $80 / ₹5,999 game
> - Stalls & rides inside → Shark Cards, GTA+ (GTA Online)
> - The few families who spend big → the ~4% of paying players (leaked)
> - Crowd brings more crowd → streamers, roleplay, 1.9B watch-hours
> - Ground owner's rent → Sony/Microsoft's ~30% cut
> - Numaish comes every year → GTA 5 still selling ~54,000 copies a day

The game price is the entry ticket. GTA Online is the exhibition inside. The 4% are those big-spending families. Streamers are the crowd that brings more crowd. And Sony and Microsoft are the ground owners collecting rent. That's why GTA isn't a product you buy once. It's a platform that keeps paying for 10 years.

## [10:55–12:20] B13 · Brands and the myth

Now a lot of people think, "Bro, such a huge game, it must be making crores from brand ads inside the game." But that's not it. Inside GTA 6 there are zero real brands. Take-Two's CEO, Strauss Zelnick, said it clearly: no product placement. "It's a fictional world… all the brands are made up," and he said this "keeps us pure".

Instead, GTA makes fun of real brands. "Sprunk" is Sprite. "eCola" is Coca-Cola. "Cluckin' Bell" is like KFC. "Lifeinvader" is Facebook. "iFruit" is Apple. Even the cars: "Pegassi" is like Lamborghini. Why? Because GTA is satire, and no real brand wants its logo next to crime and police chases.

> On screen: Sprunk = Sprite · eCola = Coca-Cola · Cluckin' Bell = KFC · Lifeinvader = Facebook · iFruit = Apple · Pegassi = Lamborghini

But outside the game, big brands are lining up. Atlantic Records released an official GTA 6 album with 34 original tracks, with artists like Travis Scott and Keith Richards. Nike and Travis Scott launched GTA 6 Air Force 1 sneakers at 130 dollars. Miami's NBA team, the Miami Heat, partnered with Rockstar, and reportedly Rockstar paid Miami-Dade around a million dollars, almost ₹9 crore, to put a giant "Welcome to Vice City" sign on top of the arena. And even brands that had no deal, like Burger King, KitKat and Netflix, made GTA-style posts just to ride the hype for free.

> On screen: Atlantic Records album (34 tracks) · Nike × Travis Scott AF1, $130 · Miami Heat · "Welcome to Vice City" sign (~$975k, reported)

And here's the other side, because I don't want to show you only the shiny side. Even with all this, Take-Two had 6.72 billion dollars in bookings in the year ending March 2026, and still posted a net loss of 298 million dollars, because of accounting from old acquisitions and the huge spending on GTA 6 before it earned anything. Gaming is a big-money game, but it's also a big-risk game.

## [12:20–15:40] B14–B12 · The future: the gaming industry and India

So now the big question: what does GTA 6 mean for the next 10 years of gaming?

First, understand the size. In 2025 the global gaming industry made 201.6 billion dollars. Global box office made around 33.6 billion, and recorded music around 31.7 billion. That means gaming is around three times bigger than movies and music combined. And around 3.6 billion people play games, almost every second human.

> On screen: Games $201.6B vs box office $33.6B + music $31.7B (≈ 3×) · 3.6B players

Analysts expect it to reach around 234 billion dollars by 2028, and PwC says around 300 billion by 2029, if you include ads. And if it keeps growing at around 5% a year, that's my own rough calculation, by 2035 we could be looking at roughly 330 to 400 billion dollars.

What's driving it? AI is changing how games are made: around 40% of game developers already use AI tools at work, though 55% of them think AI is hurting the industry. Games where players build things are paying real money: Roblox creators earned more than 1 billion dollars in just nine months of 2025. But the top creators take most of it. The median creator earned only around 1,440 dollars. So the money is real, but it goes to the people who are really good. And GTA 6 just set new rules. The 80-dollar price became normal. Other big games moved their release dates just to avoid GTA 6. And in 2026 a Saudi-led group bought EA, the maker of FIFA, for 55 billion dollars. But it's not all good news: around 11,000 game industry jobs were cut in 2026 alone. Big money and big risk, both.

> On screen: $234B by 2028 (Newzoo) · ~$300B by 2029 (PwC) · ~$330–400B by 2035 (our extrapolation)

Now let's come to India. India has more than 50 crore gamers. That's more than the entire population of America. India is number one in the world in mobile game downloads. But here's the twist: India's game spending is only around 1 to 1.5 billion dollars, less than 1% of the world's gaming money. Huge players, tiny spending. That gap is the opportunity.

> On screen: India 50 crore+ gamers > US population · but <1% of global spending

And India is changing fast. In August 2025, the government passed the Online Gaming Act and banned real-money games. Dream11 reportedly lost around 95% of its revenue overnight. But at the same time, esports was officially recognised as a sport, and real video games actually grew around 17%. The government also started the Indian Institute of Creative Technology in Mumbai with ₹400 crore, on the lines of the IITs and IIMs, for gaming, VFX and animation. And Free Fire, banned in 2022, came back as Free Fire India in October 2026.

And Indian studios are trying. *Indus*, a battle royale made in Pune, got more than 1 million downloads on day one. *Raji*, made in Pune, showed that an Indian mythology game can work worldwide. And *The Age of Bhaarat*, inspired by the Ramayana, is being pitched as India's first global AAA game. Now just think once: if India's 50 crore players start paying even a little, and Indian studios start making world-class games, how big can this get?

And the GTA connection? Rockstar has a studio in Bengaluru, Rockstar India. It used to be an Indian studio called Dhruva Interactive, and Rockstar bought it in 2019 for around 7.9 million dollars. Indian artists there worked on GTA 6's world. And Hyderabad? EA has one of its biggest studios here. *Asura*, one of the first Indian PC games to go global, was made by a Hyderabad studio. Telangana was the first state in India with a gaming and animation policy, and IndiaJoy is happening here from December 7 to 13.

> On screen: Rockstar India, Bengaluru (ex-Dhruva, $7.9M, 2019) · EA Hyderabad · *Asura* (Hyderabad) · IndiaJoy 7–13 Dec

So what should you watch next?
- **February 2027:** Take-Two's results, the first real revenue number for GTA 6.
- **GTA 6 Online:** not announced yet, but that's where the 10-year money will come from.
- **The PC version:** still not announced, and for India that matters a lot, because most Indian gamers play on mobile and PC, not on a ₹70,000 PS5 setup.
