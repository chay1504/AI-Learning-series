## [00:45–02:15] Part 1 · B8 · The global and US numbers

Let's start with the numbers. According to the tracker Layoffs.fyi, around 1,35,000 tech employees have been laid off in 2026 so far. That's already more than the whole of 2025.

And look at the big names. Amazon cut 16,000 corporate jobs in January, around 30,000 since October 2025. Oracle cut around 21,000 people, about 13% of its company, and the twist is why: to free up money for AI data centres, where its spending jumped to 55.7 billion dollars. Microsoft cut around 4,800 in July, and for the first time in 10 years its total headcount actually went down. Cisco, Intel, Canva: the list keeps going.

> On screen: Layoffs.fyi ~135,000 (2026 YTD) > all of 2025 · Amazon 16,000 · Oracle ~21,000 (13%) · Microsoft ~4,800, first headcount fall in a decade

But which roles are actually getting hit? A Stanford study looked at young workers aged 22 to 25 in the jobs most exposed to AI, and their employment has fallen behind other workers by 19%. Last year that gap was 13%. The clearest examples are software developers and customer support. Remember Salesforce? It cut its support team from 9,000 to 5,000, and the CEO literally said, "I need less heads."

And here's what a lot of people miss: software job postings in the US actually went up around 15% after AI coding tools came. But 71% of that growth is for senior roles. That means the companies still want engineers. They just want fewer beginners.

> On screen: Stanford: AI-exposed workers aged 22–25, employment gap 13% → 19% · Indeed: SWE postings +15%, but 71% of growth is senior

But here's the plot twist. In October 2026, companies blamed AI for only 1,265 job cuts, the lowest month of the year, and "economic conditions" became the number one reason. Even Sam Altman admitted some companies are doing "AI washing", blaming AI for cuts that are really about cost. So remember: it's AI plus cost-cutting plus slow demand, all together.

## [05:20–06:30] Part 2 · Move 1 · Become AI-native in your current job

Number one: become AI-native in the job you already have. Google says around 75% of its new code is now generated by AI and then reviewed by engineers. In late 2024 that was just 25%. And on LinkedIn's fastest-growing jobs list, AI Engineer is number one in the US, and it's the fastest-growing job in Hyderabad too.

> On screen: Google: ~75% of new code AI-generated (from 25% in late 2024) · LinkedIn: AI Engineer #1 (US) · fastest-growing in Hyderabad

Think of auto drivers in Hyderabad when ride-hailing apps came. The ones who learned the app kept getting rides. The ones who just waited at the stand waited longer. AI is that app for your career.

So in the next 6 months: use an AI coding agent every single day. Learn how to call LLM APIs, how to build a RAG app, which means an AI that answers from your own documents, and how agents use tools. And first, automate your own boring, repetitive work. If AI is going to take a task, make sure you're the one who automated it.

And if you're not a developer, if you're in testing, support, HR or sales, this applies to you too. One labour-market study found that more than half of the job postings asking for AI skills are now outside IT. So learn how AI is used in your own function. A support person who builds an AI helpdesk flow is the person the company keeps.

## [06:30–07:40] Part 2 · Move 2 · Learn the new skill stack: context → loop → evals

Number two: learn the new skill stack. Remember "prompt engineering", the 200,000-dollar job everyone talked about in 2023? Indeed now says postings for that title are "flatlining". Prompting became a basic skill.

What replaced it? First, context engineering, a term Shopify's CEO and Andrej Karpathy made popular in 2025: giving the AI the right documents, data and memory, not just a clever prompt. And the newest one, loop engineering, a term that's barely five months old. Simply put, you build a loop: the AI agent tries the task, a checker like automatic tests says pass or fail, and the agent keeps retrying until it passes or hits a limit. It started from a famous one-line script developers call the "Ralph Wiggum loop".

> On screen: prompt engineering → context engineering (2025) → loop engineering (June 2026) · agent tries → checker: pass/fail → retry → stop

And here's the hidden opportunity. In loop engineering, the checker is the bottleneck. So if you're in QA or testing today, and testing is one of the jobs getting automated, pivot to AI evals: testing whether AI systems are accurate and safe. Same testing brain, new market.

Practical step for this month: take one small task, write clear automatic tests for it, and let a coding agent loop on it until the tests pass. You'll learn more in that one weekend than in ten YouTube tutorials. Even AI training work is moving this way: xAI cut around 500 general data labellers but said it would grow expert tutors 10 times. The simple work goes; the expert checking grows.

## [07:40–08:45] Part 2 · Move 3 · Move closer to the customer and the business

Number three: move closer to the customer. The hottest new job title right now is FDE, Forward Deployed Engineer. An engineer who goes to the client, understands their messy problem, and makes the AI actually work there. Half engineer, half consultant. In India, FDE job postings grew around 19 times in one year, according to Indeed. Globally, one tracker measured over 1,000% growth, while all tech jobs grew just 13%. Even OpenAI has listed an applied AI engineer role in Delhi.

> On screen: FDE · India postings ~19× in a year (Indeed) · global >1,000% vs 13% for all tech (Lightcast)

And it's happening at the top too. 76% of companies now have a Chief AI Officer, a CAIO. A year ago it was only 26%. And a Telugu techie, Hariprasad Rebala, became Chief AI Officer of Sonata Software in August.

So in the next 6 months, pair your coding with communication and domain knowledge. "I know Python and I understand banking" beats "I know Python". Practise explaining a technical solution to a non-technical person, because that's literally the FDE job. And if you're senior, look at AI product manager or AI governance roles. Every company with a Chief AI Officer needs people under them who understand both the business and the rules around AI, and there are certifications like IAPP's AIGP for AI governance.

> On screen: CAIO: 76% of companies (from 26%) · IBM CEO Study 2026 · Hariprasad Rebala, CAIO, Sonata Software

## [08:45–09:30] Part 2 · Move 4 · Build proof of work, not just certificates

Number four: build proof, not just certificates. In a survey of US hiring professionals, 60% said AI makes it harder to judge a candidate's real skills. So they want to see what you've built. And AI skills pay: one study found job postings asking for AI skills pay 28% more, and 43% more if you have two or more AI skills. In India, AI roles pay around 20% more at the fresher level, and 38% more for people with 10-plus years of experience.

> On screen: 60% of hiring pros: AI makes skills harder to judge (WGU) · AI skills pay +28%, +43% with 2+ AI skills (Lightcast) · India: +20% (0–3 yrs), +38% (10+ yrs)

So here's your 6-month portfolio. Build 3 projects:
- one AI app on your own domain, like banking or healthcare;
- one agent that has tests or evals checking its work;
- one write-up of how you'd deploy it for a real customer.

Put them on GitHub and post about them on LinkedIn. Certifications are fine for clearing filters, but projects get you the interview.
