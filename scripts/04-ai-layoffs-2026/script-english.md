## [01:10–02:15] Act 1 · The year the giants changed

It all started in January 2026. Amazon cut 16,000 corporate jobs, around 30,000 since October 2025, saying it wanted to "remove layers". Then in February came Block. Its founder fired nearly half the company and said, "We're not in trouble. Our business is strong." And instead of panicking, investors clapped. The stock went up 24%. Just think about that once: the market rewarded a company for cutting half its people.

Then Oracle cut around 21,000 jobs. And the reason is the real twist: it needed money to build AI data centres. In simple words, they let people go to pay for computers.

> On screen: timeline: Jan Amazon 16,000 · Feb Block ~half, stock +24% · Oracle ~21,000 → AI data centres

Then in May, Meta cut 8,000 and moved 7,000 into AI. In July, Microsoft's total headcount fell for the first time in 10 years. By November, according to the tracker Layoffs.fyi, around 1.35 lakh tech employees had been laid off in 2026, already more than the whole of last year.

So is it all because of AI? Here's the twist. In October, American companies blamed AI for only around 1,265 job cuts, the lowest month of the year. Even Sam Altman admitted some companies are "AI washing": blaming AI for cuts that are really about saving money. So the truth is AI plus cost-cutting plus slow business, all mixed together.

> On screen: Layoffs.fyi ~1.35 lakh (2026) > all of 2025 · Oct 2026: AI blamed for only ~1,265 cuts · "AI washing"

## [02:15–03:30] Act 2 · What actually got cut: Level 1

But here's what nobody tells you. AI didn't cut "jobs". It cut tasks, and mostly the repetitive tasks done at the bottom of the pyramid.

Remember, in my IT jobs video, I explained the levels in a company? Level 1 freshers doing repetitive work, Level 2 engineers, Level 3 managers, Level 4 client heads. AI hit Level 1 first.

Look at Salesforce. It cut its customer support team from 9,000 to 5,000, and the CEO literally said, "I need less heads." A Stanford study found that young workers aged 22 to 25 in AI-exposed jobs are now 19% behind everyone else in employment. And in the US, when software job postings grew again, 71% of that growth was for senior roles. Companies still want engineers. They just want fewer beginners.

> On screen: pyramid graphic: Level 1 (repetitive tasks) highlighted red · Salesforce support 9,000 → 5,000 · Stanford: young workers −19% gap · 71% of new SWE growth = senior

And in India it's the same story. TCS announced around 12,000 cuts, but around 30,900 people left in six months. India's top 5 IT companies grew revenue around 11%, but in a full year they added only around 9,320 people, across 15 and a half lakh employees. More work, same people. Wipro said it has "no target" for fresher hiring this year. And around 1 in 3 fresher jobs became an internship or a contract. Even the Nasscom president said basic coding and testing are being automated.

> On screen: TCS: 12,000 announced → ~30,900 left in 6 months · top 5 IT: revenue +11%, people +0.6% · 1 in 3 fresher roles → internship/contract

And Ravi? His team now uses AI to write half its test cases. His manager says, "We'll need fewer testers next year." Ravi is scared.

## [05:10–06:20] Job 1 · AI Engineer (for freshers and developers)

Job number one: AI Engineer. Simply put, a software engineer who builds products using AI models like GPT, Claude or Gemini. Not training the AI from scratch, but building apps on top of it: chatbots that answer from company documents, AI agents that do tasks on their own.

This is the number one fastest-growing job on LinkedIn in the US for the second year in a row, and it's the fastest-growing job in Hyderabad and Bengaluru too. AI engineering job postings in India grew around 59% in a year, according to LinkedIn. Freshers with strong AI projects start around ₹6 to 9 lakh, and experienced people earn a lot more.

> On screen: JOB 1, AI ENGINEER · LinkedIn #1 (US) · fastest-growing in Hyderabad & Bengaluru · India postings +59% · fresher ₹6–9 LPA

A day in this job: a bank wants a chatbot that answers loan questions from 500 policy PDFs. The AI engineer builds it, connects it to the documents, and makes sure it doesn't make things up. Who's hiring? GCCs in Hyderabad, product companies and AI startups. In the US, applied AI engineers at big tech reportedly earn a median of around 250,000 dollars.

What to learn in 6 months: Python, how to call AI model APIs, RAG, which means an AI that answers from your own documents, and how agents use tools. And one new skill called context engineering: giving the AI the right documents and data, not just a clever prompt.

## [06:20–07:30] Job 2 · AI Evals Engineer (for testers and QA)

Job number two: AI Evals Engineer. And this one is for every tester like Ravi.

When AI writes code or answers customers, who checks whether it's right? That's evals: testing whether an AI system is accurate, safe and reliable. And there's a brand-new idea called loop engineering, a term that's barely five months old. Here's how it works: an AI agent tries a task, a checker says pass or fail, and the agent keeps retrying until it passes. And experts say the checker is the bottleneck, not the AI.

> On screen: JOB 2, AI EVALS ENGINEER · loop: agent tries → checker: pass/fail → retry · "the checker is the bottleneck"

A day in this job: a company is launching an AI customer-support bot. The evals engineer writes a thousand tricky test questions, checks which answers are wrong or unsafe, and blocks the release until it's fixed. And why now? Even Square Enix, the Final Fantasy company, wants AI to do 70% of its bug testing by 2027. Manual testing is shrinking. Testing AI is growing.

So think about it: testing is getting automated, but testing AI is the new bottleneck. Same testing brain, new market. It's an emerging title, so you won't see thousands of postings yet, and that's exactly why getting in early helps. What to learn: test design, Python, and how to measure AI output. This month, give an AI coding agent a small task with clear tests, and watch it loop until they pass. That's Ravi's way out.

## [07:30–08:25] Job 3 · Forward Deployed Engineer (for developers who can talk)

Job number three: FDE, Forward Deployed Engineer. An engineer who goes to the client, understands their messy real-world problem, and makes the AI actually work there. Half engineer, half consultant.

In India, FDE job postings grew around 19 times in one year, according to Indeed. Globally, one tracker measured over 1,000% growth, while all tech jobs grew just 13%. OpenAI has even listed an applied AI engineer role in Delhi. And in the US, base salaries at top AI labs are reportedly around 200,000 to 320,000 dollars.

> On screen: JOB 3, FORWARD DEPLOYED ENGINEER · India postings ×19 (Indeed) · global >1,000% vs 13% (Lightcast) · US AI lab base ~$200K–320K (reported)

A day in this job: a hospital in Hyderabad buys an AI tool. The FDE sits with the doctors for two weeks, connects the tool to their patient records, fixes what breaks, and doesn't leave until the doctors actually use it. Palantir made this title famous; now OpenAI and Anthropic are racing to hire them.

Who should target it? Mid-level developers who are good with people. What to learn: coding plus communication plus one domain. "I know Python and I understand banking" beats "I know Python". This is exactly what Sita is doing now.

## [08:25–09:10] Job 4 · AI Product Manager (for BAs, PMs and mid-level)

Job number four: AI Product Manager. The person who decides what AI product to build, for whom, and how to measure if it's working. They don't code everything themselves, but they understand what AI can and can't do. A day in this job: should a food delivery app's AI just suggest dishes, or automatically reorder your usual biryani? The AI PM decides, sets the success number, and kills the feature if users hate it.

In one analysis of around 12,400 AI product job postings in the US, the median salary was around 195,000 dollars. But here's the catch: only 2% of those jobs were junior. So this is not a fresher job; it's a pivot for business analysts, project managers and engineers with a few years of experience. What to learn: AI basics, how to evaluate AI quality, and how to turn a business problem into an AI product.

> On screen: JOB 4, AI PRODUCT MANAGER · ~12,400 US postings · median ~$195K · only ~2% junior · pivot path for BAs/PMs

## [09:10–10:10] Job 5 · Chief AI Officer (for seniors)

And job number five, the top of the ladder: Chief AI Officer, the CAIO. The person who owns the entire AI strategy of a company. A year ago, only 26% of companies had one. Today it's 76%, according to IBM. Even US government agencies are required to have one. And a Telugu techie, Hariprasad Rebala, became the Chief AI Officer of Sonata Software in August. Indian IT is creating similar top posts: Wipro made Nagendra Bandaru the CEO of its new AI segment.

But here's the honest part: one study found nearly 3 in 4 CEOs say they themselves are the main AI decision-maker. So a CAIO has to earn real power by delivering results, not just by holding the title.

> On screen: JOB 5, CHIEF AI OFFICER · 26% → 76% of companies (IBM 2026) · US federal agencies required to have one · Hariprasad Rebala, CAIO, Sonata Software

But you don't become a CAIO overnight. The ladder looks like this: AI lead, Head of AI, Director of AI, then Chief AI Officer. Director of AI is already the second fastest-growing job in Bengaluru. And under every CAIO, there's a new team of AI governance specialists who handle the rules and risks of AI, with certifications like IAPP's AIGP. If you have 10-plus years of experience, this is your lane.

> On screen: ladder: AI Lead → Head of AI → Director of AI (#2 fastest-growing, Bengaluru) → CAIO · AI governance team (IAPP AIGP)
