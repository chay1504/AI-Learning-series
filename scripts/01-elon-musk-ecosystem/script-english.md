## [02:00–05:40] B7 · Part 1, the origin story

So let's go back. Elon Musk was born on June 28, 1971, in Pretoria, South Africa. At just 12 years old he wrote a video game called *Blastar*, and he sold that code to a magazine for 500 dollars. Twelve years old, bro. At 17 he left home and moved to Canada alone, and later he studied physics and economics at the University of Pennsylvania in America.

> On screen: 1971 Pretoria → 1984 *Blastar*, $500 → 1989 Canada → UPenn

In 1995 he got admission for a PhD at Stanford, one of the best universities in the world. Do you know how many days he stayed there? Just two days. Two days, and he quit. Why? Because the internet was just starting, and he thought: "If I don't jump in now, I'll miss this wave."

So he started a company called **Zip2** with his brother Kimbal, basically online city guides and maps for newspapers. And the story here is very interesting. They didn't have money for both a house and an office. So what did he do? He rented only the office, slept in that office itself, and took bath at the YMCA nearby. In 1999, a company called Compaq bought Zip2 for 307 million dollars, and Musk's share was around 22 million dollars, before he was even 28.

> On screen: Zip2, Compaq $307M (1999) → Musk ~$22M

Now a normal person, with 22 million dollars at 27, would just settle, right? But Musk put around 10 million dollars of it into his next company: **X.com**, an online bank. Remember this name, X.com. We'll come back to it.

X.com merged with another company called Confinity. Peter Thiel and Max Levchin were there, and they had a product called **PayPal**. And then here's the twist. In September 2000, Musk was flying to his honeymoon, and while he was in the air, his own company's board removed him as CEO. Imagine that once: you go on your honeymoon, and you come back without your company.

But the story didn't end there. In 2002, eBay bought PayPal for 1.5 billion dollars, and Musk got around 180 million dollars.

> On screen: X.com + Confinity → PayPal → eBay $1.5B (2002) → Musk ~$180M

And the people from that PayPal team, we call them the "PayPal Mafia". Peter Thiel started Palantir, Reid Hoffman started LinkedIn, and three of them started YouTube. One company, and so many future companies came out of it.

Now this is where Musk is different. With 180 million dollars in his hand, what did he do? He bet almost all of it. Around 100 million into a rocket company called **SpaceX**, around 70 million into an electric car company called **Tesla**, and around 10 million into a solar company called SolarCity, started by his cousins. That means he kept almost nothing safe for himself.

> On screen: $180M → SpaceX ~$100M · Tesla ~$70M · SolarCity ~$10M

And then came 2008, the worst year of his life. His rocket, Falcon 1, failed once, twice, three times. The recession hit America. Tesla was almost out of money. Everyone said "this man is finished".

Then what happened? On September 28, 2008, on the fourth try, the rocket reached orbit, the first privately developed liquid-fuel rocket to do that. On December 23, NASA gave SpaceX a 1.6 billion dollar contract. And the very next day, December 24, Christmas Eve, at 6 pm, Tesla's funding closed. In Musk's own words, "the last hour of the last day possible". Two Christmas miracles, back to back.

> On screen: 28 Sep 2008 orbit · 23 Dec NASA $1.6B · 24 Dec 6 pm, Tesla saved

In 2018 he left the board, later sued OpenAI, and in May 2026 a jury said he filed the case too late, so he lost.

And remember X.com? In 2017 he bought that x.com domain back from PayPal for "sentimental value". And in 2023, when he renamed Twitter, what name did he choose? X. A domain from his 1999 startup became the name of the biggest social network he owns, 24 years later.

> On screen: x.com (1999) → bought back (2017) → Twitter becomes X (2023)

## [06:00–08:30] B8 · Part 2a: Tesla

Let's start with **Tesla**. Tesla was actually founded in 2003 by Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning. Musk came in 2004, put in 6.5 million dollars out of 7.5 million, and became chairman. Their first car, the Roadster, came in 2008, and only around 2,450 of them were made. But that small car was step one of the master plan.

Then came Model S, Model 3 and Model Y. And in 2023, the Model Y became the best-selling car in the whole world, the first time ever an electric car did that. Not the best-selling electric car. The best-selling car, period.

> On screen: Model Y, world's #1 car, 2023 (~1.22M)

But here's the twist. In 2024 and 2025, Tesla's sales fell two years in a row: from 1.81 million cars in 2023 to 1.64 million in 2025. And China's BYD overtook Tesla with around 2.26 million EVs. Now if you were a normal car company, what would you do? Make more cars, cheaper cars, right?

But Tesla did the opposite. In 2026 they stopped making Model S and Model X, and in that same Fremont factory they started building humanoid robots called **Optimus**. Musk literally said that around 80% of Tesla's value in the future will come from Optimus, not from cars. Just think about that once. A car company saying cars won't be its main business.

> On screen: "~80% of Tesla's value will be Optimus", Musk, Sep 2025

And then there's self-driving. Tesla's FSD has already driven more than 17 billion miles. To give you an idea, that's like going to the Sun and coming back around 90 times. With all that data, Tesla started **Robotaxi**, cars with no driver. It started in Austin in June 2025, and now in 8 American cities there are rides without anyone sitting inside to monitor. There's even a car called **Cybercab**, with no steering wheel and no pedals, and paid rides started in Austin in September 2026.

> On screen: FSD 17B miles ≈ 90 Sun round trips · Robotaxi, 8 cities · Cybercab, no steering wheel

And just last month, on October 15, Tesla showed the new Roadster. This car drove up a ramp, went upside down, and literally flew around 21 feet in the air before landing. How? Using cold-gas thrusters from SpaceX, the rocket company. Musk says 0 to 60 in under one second, with production in 2028. Remember this: a Tesla car using SpaceX rocket parts. We'll connect it later.

And one more division people forget: energy. Tesla's giant batteries, called Megapacks, went from 14.7 gigawatt-hours in 2023 to 46.7 gigawatt-hours in 2025, more than 3 times in two years.

Now this is what's important. Musk's pay package, approved in November 2025, is worth up to 1 trillion dollars. But he gets it only if Tesla becomes an 8.5 trillion dollar company, which is around 5.7 times today, and delivers 1 million Optimus robots, 1 million robotaxis, and 20 million cars. > On screen: 2025, 165 launches · ~51% of world launches · >80% of mass to orbit

> On screen: Flight 15, ship caught by the tower (14 Nov 2026)

## [11:30–13:00] B8 · Part 2c: Neuralink

Now the third company sounds like a movie: **Neuralink**.

Neuralink makes a coin-sized chip called the Link. It goes into the skull and sits on top of the brain, and it has 1,024 electrodes on 64 threads, each thread thinner than a human hair. They are so thin that a human surgeon's hand can't put them in, so a robot called R1 sews them into the brain.

> On screen: N1 Link, coin-sized · 1,024 electrodes · 64 threads · R1 robot

To put it simply, think of it like a Bluetooth mouse you control with your thoughts instead of your hand. Neuralink is basically working on three things. I call them **Move, Speak and See**.

First, **Move**, which Neuralink calls Telepathy. People who are paralysed control a computer or phone just by thinking. The first patient, Noland Arbaugh, got the chip in January 2024 and played chess and video games using only his mind. Now patients type up to 40 words per minute just by thinking. Some patients even control a robotic arm, feeding themselves and serving a drink. And one patient moved the hand of a Tesla Optimus robot with his thoughts. Remember this, a Tesla robot controlled by a Neuralink brain.

Second, **Speak**. A man named Brad Smith has ALS and can't speak. Using Neuralink he edited a YouTube video, and the voice in that video was an AI copy of his own old voice, made using Grok, Musk's AI.

Third, **See**, called Blindsight. The idea is to give limited vision to blind people by sending signals directly to the brain, skipping the eyes. Now, honestly speaking, this one has only an FDA "breakthrough" tag. It's not approved, and as of November 2026 no human has received it yet. Musk says it's ready from the engineering side and waiting for regulators.

> On screen: three cards: MOVE (Telepathy) · SPEAK (VOICE) · SEE (Blindsight, not yet in humans)

Officially, 21 people had the chip as of January 2026; reports now say around 26, and Musk says over 30. Their goal by 2028 is chips with over 25,000 channels, around 25 times today, and as they say, "integration with AI".

## [15:45–17:15] B11 · The analogy: Ameerpet Anna's biryani empire

Suppose there's an Anna who opens one biryani hotel in Ameerpet. Business is good. But every month he's paying a lot of money to other people: lorry owners to bring rice, the gas agency, the delivery apps, the cooks.

So Anna thinks, "Why am I paying everyone? Let me build it myself." He buys his own lorries, so rice comes cheaper. He gets his own generator and gas, so power never goes off. He builds his own delivery app and his own phone network. He hires one head cook with a recipe brain who trains everybody. He brings in robots to cut onions. He even digs an underground road so his delivery bikes skip the Ameerpet traffic.

Now what happens? Every business makes the other businesses cheaper and faster. His lorries carry his rice. His app sells his biryani. The profit from the hotel pays for the robots. And his competitors? They have to pay outsiders for every single one of these things. But Anna's businesses pay each other.

Exactly the same thing is happening with Musk.
